# Gift ideas and budgets

Gift-giving should be joyful, not stressful. This chapter helps you brainstorm gift ideas, set a budget you can live with, and track spending — all with AI, all for free.

## Step 1: Make your gift list

Write down everyone you want to buy for. Keep it short. It is okay to skip people, to give homemade gifts, or to agree on no-gift holidays with family.

Example list:

- Partner
- Two kids
- Mom
- Best friend
- Coworker (Secret Santa)

## Step 2: Set a budget

Decide how much you can spend in total, then split it by person. Write it down so you do not have to remember it while shopping.

Try this AI prompt:

> "Help me make a holiday gift budget. Total budget: $200. People to buy for: partner, two kids, mom, best friend, and a $25 Secret Santa coworker gift. Suggest a dollar amount for each person and leave $20 wiggle room for wrapping and cards. Show it as a simple table."

The AI might suggest something like:

| Person | Budget | Notes |
|--------|--------|-------|
| Partner | $60 | |
| Kid 1 | $40 | |
| Kid 2 | $40 | |
| Mom | $30 | |
| Best friend | $25 | |
| Secret Santa | $25 | Fixed |
| Wrapping & cards | $20 | Wiggle room |
| **Total** | **$200** | |

Adjust the numbers until they feel right to you. This is your budget — the AI is just suggesting.

## Step 3: Brainstorm gift ideas

For each person, tell the AI what they like and your budget per person.

Prompt template:

> "I need gift ideas for [Name]. They like [hobbies or interests]. My budget is about $[amount]. Please suggest 5–7 realistic gifts I can find online or at common stores, with a one-sentence reason for each and an estimated price. Keep it low-stress and practical."

Example:

> "I need gift ideas for my mom. She likes gardening and reading mysteries. My budget is about $30. Please suggest 5–7 realistic gifts I can find online or at common stores, with a one-sentence reason for each and an estimated price."

## Step 4: Thoughtful, low-cost gift ideas

Not every good gift costs money. AI can help with these too:

- A framed photo you print at home
- A "coupon book" for babysitting, a home-cooked meal, or a walk together
- A favorite recipe written out on nice paper
- A playlist of songs that remind you of that person
- A handwritten letter saying what you appreciate about them

Prompt:

> "Give me 8 free or nearly free gift ideas for [person] who [likes/interests]. Focus on thoughtful, personal, low-cost gifts."

## Step 5: Track what you buy

Keep a running list so you do not overspend or buy the same person two gifts.

| Person | Gift idea | Store | Budget | Spent | Bought? |
|--------|-----------|-------|--------|-------|---------|
| Mom | Gardening gloves | Nursery | $20 | — | ☐ |
| Best friend | Mystery novel | Bookstore | $15 | — | ☐ |

You can keep this on paper, in your phone's notes app, or print the Gift Budget Planner from `site/planners/`.

## Gift-giving for low-energy days

If you are exhausted, shift work, or managing ADHD:

- **Buy the same gift for multiple people** when it makes sense (e.g., the same tea sampler for several coworkers).
- **Shop online once** instead of visiting many stores.
- **Gift cards are fine.** A coffee-shop gift card with a handwritten note is a real gift.
- **Ask people what they want.** It removes the guesswork, and most people appreciate it.

## AI cannot

- Know what your loved ones actually like — you do.
- Guarantee an item is in stock or will arrive on time.
- Replace your judgment about what fits your budget.

Double-check prices and delivery dates before you buy, especially near holidays.
