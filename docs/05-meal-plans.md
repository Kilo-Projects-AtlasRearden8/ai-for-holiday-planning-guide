# Meal plans

Holiday meals do not have to be elaborate. This chapter helps you plan meals, build grocery lists, use what you already have, and cook without burning out.

Cook at your energy level. Store-bought and simple are good enough.

## Step 1: Check what you already have

Look in your pantry, freezer, and fridge. Write down proteins, grains, canned goods, and spices you already own.

Prompt:

> "I have these ingredients: [list what you have]. Suggest 5 simple holiday-style meals I can make using mostly these ingredients, plus a few common extras. Include prep time and cook time for each."

## Step 2: Plan your meals

Pick meals for the days you need. A simple plan might be:

| Day | Breakfast | Lunch | Dinner |
|-----|-----------|-------|--------|
| Monday | Oatmeal | Soup and bread | Roast chicken, potatoes, carrots |
| Tuesday | Yogurt and fruit | Chicken sandwiches | Pasta with jarred sauce |

Prompt template:

> "Plan [number] days of meals for [number] people around [holiday, e.g., Christmas dinner]. Include breakfast, lunch, and dinner. One meal should be a special [holiday] dinner. Keep recipes simple, with minimal chopping and one oven where possible. Note which meals can be made ahead or reheated."

## Step 3: Grocery list

Prompt:

> "Turn this meal plan into an organized grocery list: [paste your meal plan]. Group items by store section (produce, dairy, meat, pantry, frozen). Include quantities for [number] people. Flag anything likely to be expensive so I can swap it."

Check your pantry again before you shop so you do not buy what you already have.

## Step 4: Cooking timeline

For a big meal, ask AI for a timeline working backward from when you want to eat:

Prompt:

> "I want to eat dinner at [time]. Here is my menu: [list dishes]. Make a cooking timeline starting [number] hours before dinner, including prep, cooking, reheating, and resting time. Keep it simple and mark what can be done the day before."

## Step 5: Dietary needs and allergies

Prompt:

> "I am cooking for [number] people. One person is [vegetarian/gluten-free/dairy-free/nut-free/diabetic]. Suggest [number] holiday-friendly dishes that work for everyone without making separate meals."

Always double-check ingredient labels yourself — AI cannot see the package in your hand.

## Step 6: Leftovers

Prompt:

> "I have leftover [turkey/ham/roast/vegetables]. Give me 4 simple meals to use them up within 3–4 days, with storage and reheating tips."

Food safety reminder: refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours and use within 3–4 days. When in doubt, throw it out. This is general education, not food-safety certification.

## Low-energy meal tips

- **One-pan or one-pot meals** mean less cleanup.
- **Frozen vegetables** are pre-chopped and nutritious.
- **Paper plates** for big meals are fine.
- **Accept help.** If someone offers to bring a dish, say yes and tell them what.
- **Simpler is better.** People remember time together, not how fancy the food was.
