# Travel plans

Whether you are driving across state, flying to see family, or taking a train, AI can help you build a simple travel plan so nothing is forgotten.

This chapter covers itineraries, packing lists, budgets, and what to do when plans change.

## Step 1: Write down the basics

Before you open an AI chat, jot down:

- Where you are going and when (departure and return dates)
- How you are getting there (car, plane, train, bus)
- Who is traveling (adults, kids, pets)
- Your budget for travel, lodging, and food
- Any must-haves (wheelchair access, pet-friendly, dietary needs, a car seat)

## Step 2: Build a simple itinerary

Prompt template:

> "Help me plan a [number]-day trip to [destination], leaving [date] and returning [date]. We are [driving/flying] with [who]. Please make a simple day-by-day itinerary with travel times, one or two activities per day, rest time built in, and meal suggestions. Keep it low-stress — no more than two big activities per day. Budget is about $[amount] per day for food and activities."

Example:

> "Help me plan a 3-day trip to Chicago, leaving Friday December 20 and returning Sunday December 22. We are driving with two kids. Please make a simple day-by-day itinerary with travel times, one or two activities per day, rest time built in, and meal suggestions. Keep it low-stress — no more than two big activities per day. Budget is about $60 per day for food and activities."

## Step 3: Packing list

Prompt:

> "Make a packing list for a [number]-day trip to [destination] in [month], for [who: e.g., two adults and one toddler]. Include clothes, toiletries, medicines, chargers, travel documents, kid items, and anything easy to forget. Organize it as a checklist with [ ] boxes so I can print it."

Print the Travel Packing Checklist from `site/planners/` and check items off as you pack.

## Step 4: Travel budget

Prompt:

> "Help me estimate a travel budget for a trip to [destination], [number] days, [number] people, traveling by [car/plane/train]. Include categories: transport, lodging, food, activities, and an emergency buffer of 10%. Show it as a table with estimated costs."

Keep receipts in one folder or envelope so you know what you actually spent.

## Step 5: When plans change

Travel rarely goes exactly as planned. If a flight is delayed, a car breaks down, or someone gets sick:

- Ask the AI: "My flight is delayed and I will arrive at [time] instead of [time]. Help me re-plan my first evening simply."
- Call your airline, hotel, or rental company yourself — AI cannot do that for you.
- Have a "plan B" that is just resting at your hotel or a relative's house. That is a valid plan.

## Safety and privacy reminders

- Never paste your passport number, license number, or payment card details into an AI chat.
- Do not share your exact home address or dates when your home will be empty in public posts.
- Double-check all travel times, confirmation numbers, and entry requirements yourself. AI can make mistakes about visas, border rules, and weather.

## Low-energy travel tips

- Pack a "comfort bag" with snacks, water, headphones, and anything that helps you regulate.
- Schedule one rest day before you travel and one after you return, if you can.
- If you are a shift worker or healthcare worker, plan travel around your sleep schedule, not the other way around.
- It is okay to say no to a trip. Staying home is a holiday plan too.
