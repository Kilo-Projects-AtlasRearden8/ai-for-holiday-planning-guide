Post-holiday decluttering

After the holidays, homes often feel fuller: new gifts, leftover food, wrapping paper, cards, and decorations. This chapter helps you declutter at low energy, one small step at a time.

You do not have to do it all in one day. One drawer is a win.

Step 1: Pick one small area

Do not start with the whole house. Pick one:

  • One kitchen drawer
  • The entryway where coats and bags pile up
  • One gift-wrapping supply spot
  • The fridge (leftovers first!)

Set a timer for 10 or 15 minutes. When it rings, you are allowed to stop.

Step 2: Use four piles

For anything you touch, decide: keep, donate, recycle, or trash.

  • Keep: things you use or love
  • Donate: gifts that do not fit, unopened non-perishable food, decorations you will not use
  • Recycle: wrapping paper without glitter, cards, cardboard boxes
  • Trash: broken items, food that has gone bad

Prompt for decisions you are stuck on:

"I am decluttering after the holidays. I have [item] and I cannot decide whether to keep it or donate it. Ask me 3 questions to help me decide, one at a time."

Answer the questions yourself — the AI is just asking.

Step 3: Gifts and new things

  • If you received a gift you will not use, it is okay to donate it or return it. The gift was the thought; the object is yours to decide about.
  • New toys, clothes, or gadgets often mean old ones can leave. Try a "one in, one out" rule if it helps you.
  • Gift cards: put them in your wallet or a dedicated envelope so you do not forget them.

Step 4: Food and leftovers

  • Check dates on leftover holiday food. Use, freeze, or toss.
  • Donate unopened non-perishable food to a local pantry (call first to check what they accept).
  • Wipe one shelf of the fridge at a time.

Step 5: Decorations

  • Box decorations by type (lights, ornaments, wrapping supplies) and label the boxes.
  • Test light strings before you pack them. Toss broken ones.
  • Take down one room at a time.

Step 6: Paperwork and cards

  • Holiday cards you want to keep: one box or album.
  • Receipts for gifts: keep in an envelope in case of returns, then toss after the return window closes.
  • Never paste account numbers or personal details from receipts into an AI chat.

Step 7: Make it ADHD-friendly

  • Body doubling: have a friend sit with you while you declutter, even silently.
  • Music or a podcast can make the time pass faster.
  • Clear containers let you see what is inside without opening everything.
  • Celebrate small wins. One bag for donation is progress.

When to stop

Decluttering is done when your space feels manageable to you — not when it looks like a magazine. Rest when you need to. The holidays are over; you do not need another project.
