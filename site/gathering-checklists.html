Gathering checklists

Hosting a gathering — dinner, a party, a cookie swap, or a small get-together — can be joyful and overwhelming at the same time. This chapter breaks it into small steps: guest list, prep timeline, day-of run sheet, and cleanup.

Host at your energy level. A simple gathering with store-bought food is still a good gathering.

Step 1: Decide the basics

Write down:

  • What the gathering is (birthday dinner, holiday party, cookie exchange, casual brunch)
  • Date and time
  • Where (your home, a park, a community room)
  • Rough headcount
  • Your budget

Step 2: Guest list and RSVPs

Prompt:

"Help me draft a friendly invitation for a [type of gathering] on [date] at [time] at [location]. Include: what to bring (or 'nothing, just yourself'), RSVP deadline, parking details, and whether kids or pets are welcome. Keep the tone warm and low-pressure."

Send it by text, email, or paper — whatever is easiest for you. Keep a list:

GuestContactRSVPDietary needsNotes
[Name][Phone/email]☐ Yes ☐ No

Never paste real guest names or contact details into a public AI chat — use [Guest 1] placeholders until you paste the final text yourself.

Step 3: Prep timeline (working backward)

AI is great at timelines. Tell it your event date and it will suggest what to do when.

Prompt:

"I am hosting a [type of gathering] for [number] people on [date] at [time]. Make a prep timeline working backward from the event: one week before, a few days before, the day before, the morning of, and 2 hours before guests arrive. Include shopping, cooking, cleaning, and setting the table. Keep it realistic for one person with low energy."

Example output might look like:

  • 7 days before: finalize guest count, make grocery list, buy non-perishables
  • 3 days before: buy perishables, defrost anything frozen, confirm RSVPs
  • Day before: chop vegetables, set out serving dishes, tidy the bathroom guests will use
  • Morning of: cook anything that reheats well, set the table, put out trash/recycling bins
  • 2 hours before: shower, change, light a candle if you like, take five deep breaths

You do not have to follow it perfectly. It is a guide, not a rulebook.

Step 4: Day-of run sheet

For larger gatherings, an hour-by-hour run sheet keeps you from holding everything in your head.

Prompt:

"Make a simple hour-by-hour run sheet for a [type of gathering] starting at [time]. Include: guest arrival, food serving, cake or toast if any, gift opening if any, and winding down. Keep it flexible."

Print the Gathering Run Sheet from site/planners/ and tape it to the fridge.

Step 5: Low-energy hosting tips

  • Buy instead of bake. Store-bought cookies, a veggie tray, or rotisserie chicken count as hosting.
  • Ask guests to bring something. A potluck means less cooking for you.
  • Paper plates are fine. Dishes can wait.
  • One room only. Tidy the space guests will see and close the door on the rest.
  • Sit down while you prep if standing is hard on your body.
  • Rest after. Give yourself permission to do nothing the next day.

Step 6: Cleanup plan

Before guests arrive, decide where dirty dishes, trash, and recycling go. After the gathering:

  • Load the dishwasher or soak pots and leave them overnight
  • Take out the trash
  • Wipe one surface

Everything else can wait until morning.
